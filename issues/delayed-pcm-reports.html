1What Private Click Measurement is and why reports are delayed 24–48 hours

Private Click Measurement (PCM) is Apple's proposed web standard (developed in the W3C Privacy Community Group) for measuring advertising conversions without third-party cookies or cross-site tracking. When a user clicks an ad on a publisher website, the browser stores an unattributed ad-click event (an 8-bit source ID, providing 256 values). If the user subsequently performs a conversion on the advertiser website (such as purchasing a product or registering an account), the advertiser triggers an attribution event (a 4-bit trigger value, providing 16 values, plus an optional priority).

Crucially, the browser does not send an attribution report immediately upon conversion. Instead, the specification mandates a random delay between 24 and 48 hours before dispatching the attribution report to both the publisher and advertiser endpoints. This deliberate temporal detachment is fundamental to user privacy: if the attribution request were transmitted at the instant of conversion, the publisher and advertiser could correlate the server timestamp of the conversion request with the original ad click timestamp, effectively reconstructing the user's cross-site browsing journey despite the absence of cookies.

WebKit's source code implements this in PrivateClickMeasurement::attributeAndGetEarliestTimeToSend(), setting two randomized send times: 24_h + Seconds(cryptographicallyRandomUnitInterval() * (24_h).value()). Furthermore, if multiple conversion reports are pending or overdue when the reporting timer evaluates, WebKit enforces a second layer of temporal jitter in PrivateClickMeasurementManager::randomlyBetweenFifteenAndThirtyMinutes(). By staggering overdue reports by a random 15 to 30 minutes, WebKit prevents network bursts that could otherwise reveal a batch of conversions tied to a single user awakening their device.

How Safari / WebKit does it

  • When a conversion occurs, WebKit records the conversion and calculates two independent send timestamps randomized between 24 and 48 hours in the future (one for the ad source site and one for the conversion destination site).
  • Attribution reports are stored persistently on disk. When overdue reports are ready, they are sent individually, and any additional overdue reports are staggered by a random 15–30 minute interval to avoid burst correlation.
  • Because report dispatch is offloaded to a system daemon, the 24–48 hour delay is faithfully preserved even if Safari is terminated immediately after the conversion.

What a BrowserEngineKit browser can do

  • A third-party engine can implement the exact same 24–48 hour mathematical delay calculation and 15–30 minute overdue staggering logic in its own code.
  • However, the engine can only honour the delay timer while its processes are actively running on the device. Once the user closes the app or switches away, the timing guarantees collapse.
  • If the user does not return to the browser within the 24–48 hour window, reports cannot be sent on schedule. When the user eventually reopens the browser days or weeks later, all pending reports fire belatedly, degrading advertiser measurement freshness.

  • WebKitPrivateClickMeasurement.cpp:251–269 — randomlyBetweenTwentyFourAndFortyEightHours() computes the 24–48 hour randomized delay, noting in comments: "This helps privacy since the conversion and the attribution requests are detached and the time of the attribution does not reveal the time of the conversion."
  • WebKitPrivateClickMeasurementManager.cpp:616–622 and :647–653 — randomlyBetweenFifteenAndThirtyMinutes() staggers overdue attribution reports by 15–30 minutes to eliminate traffic bursts.

2The adattributiond daemon architecture: persistent system-wide coordination

Because iOS aggressively suspends and terminates background applications, an in-process timer inside Safari or its helper processes cannot survive for 24 to 48 hours. To solve this, Apple engineered a dedicated standalone system daemon named adattributiond (installed at /System/Library/Frameworks/WebKit.framework/Daemons/adattributiond and managed by launchd under the label com.apple.webkit.adattributiond).

Instead of storing ad clicks and conversions in Safari's container or WebKit's Networking process memory, WebKit offloads persistent PCM records to adattributiond over Mach IPC via the service name com.apple.webkit.adattributiond.service. The daemon maintains a centralized, device-wide SQLite database located at /private/var/mobile/Library/com.apple.webkit.adattributiond/Version1/pcm.db. This database persists unattributed clicks (UnattributedPrivateClickMeasurement), attributed conversions (AttributedPrivateClickMeasurement), and observed registrable domains across system reboots and application lifecycles.

Access to this daemon is strictly protected by Apple's code-signing infrastructure. In DaemonUtilities.mm, the daemon's listener explicitly verifies that any connecting XPC peer possesses the Apple-private entitlement com.apple.private.webkit.adattributiond. While Apple's build script grants this entitlement to Apple's system com.apple.WebKit.Networking binary, third-party browser extensions created under BrowserEngineKit (specifically com.apple.developer.web-browser-engine.networking) do not receive it.

How Safari / WebKit does it

  • In WebsiteDataStoreConfiguration.cpp, WebKit initializes m_pcmMachServiceName = "com.apple.webkit.adattributiond.service" for all persistent data stores on iOS and visionOS.
  • WebKit's NetworkSession creates a PCM::ManagerProxy, which connects over Mach IPC to adattributiond using a custom 19-message binary protocol (PCM::MessageType).
  • The system daemon runs under a dedicated, hardened Seatbelt profile (com.apple.WebKit.adattributiond.sb.in) with direct preferences mode (_CFPrefsSetDirectModeEnabled(YES)) and exclusive read/write access to its system-wide SQLite directory.

What a BrowserEngineKit browser can do

  • A third-party browser cannot connect to com.apple.webkit.adattributiond.service. Any connection attempt from an unentitled process is immediately cancelled by the daemon's listener in DaemonUtilities.mm.
  • com.apple.private.webkit.adattributiond is an Apple-internal entitlement not granted to third-party developers under any BrowserEngineKit entitlement profile.
  • The third-party browser cannot access the shared system SQLite directory (/private/var/mobile/Library/com.apple.webkit.adattributiond) due to iOS app sandboxing, and must instead manage attribution state exclusively inside its own private container.

3Background execution and the xpc_activity gap: waking when the browser is closed

The core capability gap is not storing the attribution data—it is waking up 24 to 48 hours later to send the report when the browser application is no longer running.

Apple solves this by registering adattributiond as a system-level background activity with launchd. The daemon's launch property list (com.apple.webkit.adattributiond.plist) registers a repeating 24-hour XPC activity event named com.apple.webkit.adattributiond.activity with criteria: Interval = 86400, GracePeriod = 3600, Priority = Maintenance, AllowBattery = true, and RequireNetworkConnectivity = true. In PCMDaemonEntryPoint.mm, the daemon registers an activity handler using xpc_activity_register(). When iOS determines that network connectivity is available and the device is idle, launchd automatically spawns or wakes adattributiond in the background, executing PCM::doDailyActivityInManager() and firing all due attribution requests.

BrowserEngineKit provides no equivalent background execution facility for third-party engines. BrowserEngineKit defines exactly three extension process types: BEWebContentProcess (rendering), BEGPUProcess (media/graphics), and BENetworkingProcess (network I/O). None of these extension types can register persistent xpc_activity handlers, nor can they run as background system daemons. When a user closes or swipes away a third-party browser, iOS suspends and quickly terminates all three extension processes.

How Safari / WebKit does it

  • adattributiond is a system launch daemon with ProcessType = Adaptive, EnablePressuredExit = true, and EnableTransactions = true.
  • The daemon registers a 24-hour maintenance activity via xpc_activity_register with a randomized initial delay. iOS wakes the daemon independently of Safari.
  • When woken, doDailyActivityInManager() queries pcm.db for any attribution records whose earliest send time has passed, transmits the reports over HTTPS, and clears the sent attributions.

What a BrowserEngineKit browser can do

  • Third-party browsers are restricted to public background APIs: BGTaskScheduler (specifically BGAppRefreshTask and BGProcessingTask).
  • Public background tasks are strictly opportunistic: iOS determines when (and whether) to execute them based on user engagement frequency, battery level, and charging status. If a user rarely opens the alternative browser, iOS deprioritizes or completely suppresses its background execution.
  • Furthermore, background tasks have strict runtime budgets (typically 30 seconds for app refreshes). If the browser cannot secure background runtime within the 24–48 hour window, reports are delayed until the next interactive launch, or lost entirely if the app is eventually uninstalled.

4Privacy transport: Oblivious HTTP & iCloud Private Relay integration

Even if an attribution report is successfully delayed, transmitting it directly from the user's mobile device reveals the user's IP address to the receiving attribution endpoint (/.well-known/private-click-measurement/report-attribution/). An advertising network receiving conversion reports could combine the user's client IP address with the conversion payload to link the user's identity.

To eliminate IP-based tracking, WebKit integrates PCM reporting with Apple's Oblivious HTTP / iCloud Private Relay infrastructure. In June 2026, WebKit landed commit f8277ab0a96d ("[PCM] Support proxying PCM requests on iOS"), granting adattributiond direct access to com.apple.networkserviceproxy. When constructing outbound attribution requests in NetworkDataTaskCocoa.mm, WebKit marks non-personally-identifiable requests with request._needsNetworkTrackingPrevention = YES and request._privacyProxyFailClosed = YES. This instructs CFNetwork to blind the user's IP address by routing the conversion report through Apple's privacy proxy relays, failing closed if the proxy is unreachable.

To authorize this transport, Apple's signing script provisions adattributiond with three private entitlements: com.apple.private.networkserviceproxy, com.apple.security.exception.mach-lookup.global-name = ["com.apple.networkserviceproxy"], and com.apple.private.network.socket-delegate. In late June 2026, commit 587814c5e938 added (remote udp) outbound network permissions to the daemon's sandbox profile, enabling HTTP/3 QUIC transport over Private Relay. None of these privacy proxy entitlements or Mach services are accessible to third-party browsers.

How Safari / WebKit does it

  • adattributiond configures an ephemeral, stateless NSURLSession with HTTPCookieAcceptPolicyNever, no cookie storage, and no URL cache.
  • Outbound conversion reports set _needsNetworkTrackingPrevention = YES and _privacyProxyFailClosed = YES, delegating socket accounting to com.apple.mobilesafari and routing traffic through Apple's Oblivious HTTP / Private Relay network.
  • Reports are sent via HTTP/2 or HTTP/3 QUIC without disclosing the client device's real IP address to the advertiser or publisher.

What a BrowserEngineKit browser can do

  • A third-party browser cannot route traffic through com.apple.networkserviceproxy. The Mach service and the underlying entitlements are strictly reserved for Apple system daemons.
  • If a third-party browser sends attribution reports directly over standard HTTPS, the user's IP address is exposed to the destination endpoint, compromising the privacy guarantees of the attribution protocol.
  • To match Safari's privacy posture, a third-party browser vendor must design, deploy, and finance its own multi-hop Oblivious HTTP relay proxy network across global data centers, imposing significant ongoing infrastructure costs.

Evidence

5Cryptographic parity: RSA-BSSA blind signatures in userland

To prevent fraudulent attribution reports while preserving anonymity, Private Click Measurement incorporates cryptographic unlinkable tokens using RSA Blind Signatures for Anonymous Credentials (RSA-BSSA, standardized in RFC 9474). When an ad click or conversion trigger occurs, the browser contacts the destination server to fetch a public key, blinds a random nonce, obtains a signature from the server, and unblinds it. The resulting secret token proves that the attribution was authorized without allowing the server to link the token to the original user session.

Inside WebKit, PrivateClickMeasurementCocoa.mm implements token blinding by soft-linking Apple's private framework CryptoKitPrivate.framework, specifically instantiating RSABSSATokenBlinder, RSABSSATokenWaitingActivation, and RSABSSATokenReady. Under App Store review guidelines, third-party applications are prohibited from linking private frameworks like CryptoKitPrivate.

Public cryptographic parity: Unlike background execution or network proxying, cryptography does not depend on kernel privileges or private Mach services. Because RSA-BSSA is an open mathematical standard (RFC 9474), third-party browser engines (such as Chromium with BoringSSL or Gecko with NSS) already bundle standard cryptographic libraries capable of computing RSA blind signatures in userland. Consequently, fraud prevention token generation represents zero functional capability loss for alternative engines.

How Safari / WebKit does it

  • WebKit soft-links private symbols from CryptoKitPrivate.framework via CryptoKitPrivateSoftLink.mm.
  • Calls PAL::allocRSABSSATokenBlinderInstance() to generate blinded tokens and verifies cryptographic signatures against server public keys.
  • The private framework dependency is an internal implementation choice rather than a capability barrier.

What a BrowserEngineKit browser can do

  • A third-party engine cannot link CryptoKitPrivate.framework.
  • However, the engine can execute identical RFC 9474 RSA-BSSA token blinding, signing, and verification using its own bundled cryptographic libraries (e.g. BoringSSL, OpenSSL, or Rust cryptography crates).
  • Full cryptographic parity is achieved entirely in-engine without any system daemon or private entitlement requirements.

Evidence

  • WebKitPrivateClickMeasurementCocoa.mm:50–82 — invokes PAL::allocRSABSSATokenBlinderInstance() and tokenWaitingActivationWithContent: for unlinkable token calculation.

6WebKit's in-engine fallback: the PrivateClickMeasurementManager SQLite store

An illuminating detail in WebKit's source code is that WebKit still maintains a complete, fully functional, in-process Private Click Measurement implementation: PrivateClickMeasurementManager paired with PrivateClickMeasurementDatabase. The out-of-process daemon is actually an iOS-specific specialization.

In NetworkSession.cpp, the helper function managerOrProxy() evaluates how PCM should be initialized. If parameters.pcmMachServiceName is non-empty and the session is not ephemeral, it returns PCM::ManagerProxy::create(), connecting to adattributiond. However, if pcmMachServiceName is empty—which is the default on macOS (where adattributiond is not installed)—or if the session is an ephemeral (Private Browsing) data store, WebKit instantiates PrivateClickMeasurementManager directly inside the NetworkProcess.

This in-process manager runs its own SQLite database (or ephemeral memory store) using the exact same schema and timer logic as the daemon. It maintains a RunLoop::Timer (m_firePendingAttributionRequestsTimer) to check for due attributions and execute HTTP reporting. This provides the exact architectural blueprint for how a third-party BrowserEngineKit engine must operate on iOS.

How Safari / WebKit does it

  • On iOS and visionOS, persistent data stores configure pcmMachServiceName = "com.apple.webkit.adattributiond.service", routing all attribution events out-of-process to the system daemon.
  • On macOS and in private browsing sessions, WebKit bypasses the daemon entirely and runs PrivateClickMeasurementManager in-process inside com.apple.WebKit.Networking.

What a BrowserEngineKit browser can do

  • A third-party engine on iOS must adopt the architecture WebKit uses on macOS: host an in-process attribution store and timer manager inside its own BENetworkingProcess or application container.
  • The engine records clicks and conversions to a local SQLite database, sets in-memory timers while running, and inspects the database for overdue reports on every application launch.
  • While functionally identical when the browser is actively used, this in-process approach inevitably exposes the browser to the process suspension and lifecycle limitations described in aspect 3.

Evidence

  • WebKitNetworkSession.cpp:117–130 — managerOrProxy() branches: returns PCM::ManagerProxy if pcmMachServiceName is provided, or constructs an in-process PrivateClickMeasurementManager.
  • WebKitWebsiteDataStoreConfiguration.cpp:81–83 — m_pcmMachServiceName is explicitly gated with #if PLATFORM(IOS) || PLATFORM(VISION).
  • WebKitPrivateClickMeasurementDatabase.cpp:41–65 and :93–101 — SQLite schema and database opening for pcm.db.

Open questions & confidence

  • Confidence is High: The source code for adattributiond, its launchd plist, the Mach service entitlement gate, the 24–48 hour randomized delay algorithm, and the fail-closed Private Relay transport are all publicly inspectable in the open-source WebKit repository.
  • Entitlement barrier is absolute: The gate in DaemonUtilities.mm checking for com.apple.private.webkit.adattributiond is hardcoded in WebKit daemon code. Third-party developers cannot acquire this entitlement or communicate with adattributiond.service.
  • Real-world attribution loss: The extent to which third-party browsers lose conversion reports depends heavily on end-user behaviour. For users who employ a third-party browser as their primary default browser, foreground app opens occur frequently enough that overdue reports will usually fire within several days. However, for secondary browsers or infrequently launched apps, attribution reports may sit in local SQLite storage for weeks or be purged if the user deletes the app before reopening it.
  • Attribution Reporting API (ARA) vs. PCM: Other modern engines (like Chromium) prioritize the W3C Attribution Reporting API (ARA) rather than WebKit's Private Click Measurement. While ARA supports more complex aggregation keys and flexible report windows, the fundamental iOS background execution problem remains identical: any attribution API requiring multi-day delayed reporting cannot fire reliably on iOS without a system-level background daemon.
  • Sibling gap comparison: Note that web-to-web ad attribution (PCM / ARA) discussed here is technically distinct from web-to-app install attribution. Web-to-app attribution relies on Apple's native StoreKit / SKAdNetwork daemon (storekitd / adprivacyd), which involves different system frameworks and App Store transaction records (see Web-to-app attribution).

Android comparison

On Android, browsers have two viable paths for executing delayed ad-attribution reporting, neither of which leaves alternative engines at a competitive disadvantage compared to the system browser:

  1. Internal browser background scheduling: Android does not immediately freeze background applications or prohibit them from scheduling future background work. Chrome on Android implements the W3C Attribution Reporting API using internal storage paired with Android's system JobScheduler and WorkManager (via Chromium's BackgroundTaskScheduler). Any third-party browser engine on Android can register identical background jobs with the operating system to wake the browser engine after 24 to 48 hours to dispatch pending attribution reports.
  2. System-level Privacy Sandbox Attribution Reporting: Starting with Android 13, Google introduced the system-level Privacy Sandbox on Android (part of the modular system AdServices package). The OS exposes a public MeasurementManager API that allows both native apps and web browsers to register attribution sources and triggers directly with the Android platform. When using this API, the Android OS manages noise injection, randomized delay windows, and background network delivery at the operating system level, providing an exact functional equivalent to Apple's adattributiond that is equally accessible to all browsers.

Consequently, on Android, alternative browser engines can either schedule their own reliable background delivery or delegate to the OS attribution service. On iOS, third-party engines are barred from the OS daemon and denied reliable background execution triggers.
